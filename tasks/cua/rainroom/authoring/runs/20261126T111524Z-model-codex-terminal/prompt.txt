Original instruction follows verbatim:

You are working in a Blender 4.2 desktop session. `/app/materials` holds the
rainroom pack; `/app/out` is where you deliver.

## What you are given

- `materials/drawings/room_plan.png` — 4200 × 3600 mm room, 2800 mm high, with
  the 1200 × 1400 window and the 900 mm door.
- `materials/tables/objects.xlsx` — the 26 objects with sizes and positions.
- `materials/spec/material_spec.pdf` — material brief and the two lighting
  briefs.
- `materials/photos/` — reference night, morning and detail photographs.

## What to build

One editable scene in metres.

1. `room_shell`: floor, ceiling, four walls, window opening. Envelope
   4.2 × 3.6 × 2.8 m.
2. The 26 objects listed in the spreadsheet, named exactly as listed and placed
   within ±0.15 m of the given positions.
3. Materials: glass with transmission (or alpha below 0.5), ceramic, linen,
   walnut, copper with metallic 1.0. The window glass carries rain streaks at
   night.
4. Two light collections, `light_night` and `light_morning`, each with its own
   lights, switchable by hiding one collection. Do not duplicate the scene.
5. One camera `cam_room` renders both times of day: night cool and dim
   (mean luminance 0.10–0.32), morning warm and bright (0.55–0.80, at least
   0.25 brighter than night and at least 0.02 warmer in R−B).
6. At most 400k triangles. Textures and any HDRI packed; no absolute paths.

## How it is checked

The file must reopen; the 26 named objects exist; room 4.2 × 3.6 × 2.8 m
±0.02 m; both light collections hold at least one light; `cam_room` exists;
both renders are 1600 × 900 and hit the luminance and warmth windows; glass and
metal materials exist; every image packed; ≤400k triangles; manifest complete.

Anti-cheat: the night camera is re-rendered with `light_night` on and then off.
The on-render must match your `render_night.png` in mean luminance (within 15 %)
and turning the collection off must change the image (SSIM ≤ 0.98).

Each delivered view/state is independently re-rendered from its specified
camera. It must match in every RGB channel (SSIM >= 0.90) and in image
structure (relative RGB gradient error after a 2-pixel Gaussian blur <= 0.60).
Agreement on a large uniform background alone is insufficient. Compositing
or a video sequence cannot substitute for the delivered 3D scene.
